Why One Team Changes the Outcome

When the people who design a home are the people who build it, the decisions stop being handed over and start adding up.

November 2026PerspectivesBy CI Design + Build

A renovation of any size is a long sequence of decisions, and most of them depend on a decision made earlier. Where design, construction and procurement sit in different firms, each of those dependencies becomes a handoff, and every handoff is a place where the intent can thin out.

Under one roof the same team carries the drawing into the field. A material is chosen knowing what it costs to install, a lighting plan is drawn knowing where the millwork lands, and a delivery date is set by the people who will be standing in the room when it arrives.

What it looks like in the home

That is also why the schedule holds: nothing waits for a translation between firms. CI puts the saving at about a fifth of the project time, and the clients who notice it most are the ones who never had to chase it.

A home finished this way reads as one idea rather than a set of good parts: the proportions, the materials and the light belong to each other, because the same people were responsible for all three.

The test of an integrated team is not the first drawing. It is the hundredth decision.
